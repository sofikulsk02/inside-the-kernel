# History of Operating Systems

## Early Days (1950s)

The first operating systems were simple batch systems that ran on massive mainframe computers. They executed jobs sequentially from punched cards or tape, with minimal user interaction.

## Multiprogramming Era (1960s)

Operating systems evolved to support **multiprogramming**, allowing multiple jobs to reside in memory simultaneously. IBM's OS/360 and MIT's **CTSS** (Compatible Time-Sharing System, 1961) introduced time-sharing, letting multiple users interact with a computer at once.

## The Rise of UNIX (1970s)

**UNIX**, developed at Bell Labs by Ken Thompson and Dennis Ritchie, became the most influential OS of the era. Written in C, it introduced concepts like hierarchical filesystems, pipes, and a modular design that shaped modern operating systems.

## Personal Computing (1980s)

- **MS-DOS** (1981) — Microsoft's disk operating system for IBM PCs.
- **Mac OS** (1984) — Apple introduced the graphical user interface (GUI) to mainstream users.
- **GNU Project** (1983) — Richard Stallman launched the free software movement.

## Modern Era (1990s–Today)

- **Linux** (1991) — Linus Torvalds released the Linux kernel, spawning countless distributions.
- **Windows 95** — Brought the Start menu and Plug-and-Play to the masses.
- **macOS X, Android, iOS** — Mobile and consumer OSes now dominate computing worldwide.
